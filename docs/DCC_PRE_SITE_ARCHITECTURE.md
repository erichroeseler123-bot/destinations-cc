# Destination Command Center: Pre-Site Architecture

## Core role

Destination Command Center (DCC) is the **pre-site for the suite**.

DCC owns the pages a traveler is likely to need **before** they are ready to transact on one of the specialist sites.

DCC should answer the question, explain the tradeoffs, reduce uncertainty, and identify the next move. Only then should it hand the traveler to a focused specialist or transaction site.

The specialist sites are not satellites that repeat DCC content. They are the places where the traveler chooses, books, requests, reserves, or completes the next action.

## Funnel

**Search / AI / direct discovery**

→ **DCC: UNDERSTAND**

→ **specialist site: CHOOSE**

→ **transaction site / operator: BUY OR RESERVE**

→ **Cruise Promenade where relevant: PLAN AND SHARE**

## What belongs on DCC

DCC should own content such as:

- What should I know before I book this?
- Is this realistic in the time I have?
- What are the tradeoffs between option A and option B?
- How much return buffer do I need?
- What happens if weather changes?
- What happens if traffic or parking fails?
- What can I do in this cruise port in 4, 6, 8, or 10 hours?
- Is a rental car, shuttle, rideshare, or private transfer the better move?
- Which tour type fits my group?
- What should I do if I arrive with no plan?
- What is the backup if the first choice cancels?
- What should families, older travelers, mobility-limited travelers, or large groups know?
- What changes by season, ship schedule, venue, neighborhood, or airport?

DCC pages must be useful even if the visitor never clicks a downstream link.

## What does NOT belong on DCC

DCC should not become:

- a generic OTA
- a duplicate catalog of products sold elsewhere in the suite
- a giant list of thin destination pages
- a doorway-page network
- a copy of the specialist sites
- a fake booking interface
- a page whose primary value is an outbound link

## Handoff rule

A downstream link should answer the question:

> "Now that I understand the decision, where do I actually do the thing?"

The handoff should be contextual. Good examples:

- DCC explains DEN → Vail transportation tradeoffs → GoSno handles the private-transfer booking.
- DCC explains airboat vs covered swamp boat → Welcome to New Orleans Tours / Welcome to the Swamp handles choosing and booking the experience.
- DCC explains Juneau port-day timing, flight-weather risk, and backup logic → Juneau Flight Deck handles glacier-flight comparison and next booking step.
- DCC explains Red Rocks transportation failure modes → Party at Red Rocks or Red Rocks Fast Pass handles the ride.
- DCC explains what a cruise group can realistically do in port → Cruise Promenade can hold the resulting plan.
- DCC explains independent local-driver touring at a cruise port → Vibe Around Town handles the driver marketplace request.

## Content quality standard

Every important DCC page should contain five things:

1. **The decision** — what the traveler is actually trying to figure out.
2. **The constraints** — time, location, weather, traffic, mobility, group size, cost, provider terms, or schedule.
3. **The comparison** — what changes between reasonable options.
4. **The evidence** — sourced facts, operating details, structured data, or clearly identified network knowledge.
5. **The next move** — what to do next, including a specialist handoff only when it is useful.

## Authority strategy

DCC should aim to become a source, not merely look like one.

Priority assets include structured, maintainable knowledge about:

- cruise port schedules and usable port time
- ship arrival/departure windows
- destination travel times
- transportation modes
- venue arrival/departure constraints
- excursion durations
- weather sensitivity
- cancellation / backup behavior
- pickup logistics
- accessibility and group-fit considerations
- seasonal operating constraints
- practical failure modes

The strongest DCC pages should combine this structured knowledge with clear human-readable analysis.

## Network SEO rule

The suite should not publish substantially identical pages across multiple domains.

DCC owns the **pre-transaction question and analysis**.

The specialist owns the **focused choice and merchandising**.

The transaction site owns the **execution**.

Cross-links should be sparse, relevant, and tied to the user's next action rather than inserted solely for SEO.

## Simple test

Before publishing any page in the suite, ask:

**Is the visitor still trying to understand what to do?**
→ DCC.

**Do they understand the problem and now need to choose within a category or destination?**
→ specialist site.

**Are they ready to reserve, request, or buy?**
→ transaction site.

**Do they need to organize and share the resulting cruise plan?**
→ Cruise Promenade.
